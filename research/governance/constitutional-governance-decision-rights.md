## Why Governance survives as its own study

Authority answers whether a concrete effect is authorized. Governance is broader: who may define policy, accept residual risk, change architecture, grant exceptions, contest decisions and change the rules by which those decisions are made.

## Decision rights ≠ permissions

An actor may have filesystem capability and a Permit to write a file without having the institutional right to decide that a public contract or canonical primitive should change.

Separate:

- capability — can the mechanism do it?
- Authority — is this concrete effect authorized?
- decision right — is this actor legitimate to choose the desired state?
- responsibility — who operates/executes?
- accountability — who answers for the decision/outcome?
- assurance — what evidence is required?

## Constitutional chain hypothesis

```text
Root Authority
 → organization mandate
 → Project mandate
 → delegations
 → Permits
```

This is RESEARCH framing, not a ratified object model.

## Policy lifecycle

Ordinary policy changes, architecture-boundary changes and constitutional/root changes should not necessarily share the same amendment process. Preserve policy epoch, non-retroactivity of historical judgments, scoped/expiring emergency authority, reasoned waivers and post-event review.

## Three Lines adaptation

First line operates/owns risk; second line designs/challenges policy/risk/assurance; third line independently audits. Governing authority sets objectives/risk appetite/decision rights. This prevents Audit from becoming management.
