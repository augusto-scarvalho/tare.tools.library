Scientific & Exploratory Research · 2026-08-11

Cyclic Multi-Role Research Protocol

A self-applied study of staged same-model research roles, adversarial revision, evidence limits and the path toward true heterogeneous research workflows.

RESEARCHPROPOSED METHODSAME-MODEL RUNNOT INDEPENDENT
Authority boundary. This study is research methodology evidence. It does not grant Agent OS authority, strict assurance or implementation permission.
Run outcome. ADAPT / ACCEPT_FOR_EXPERIMENTAL_SHADOW_USE. Comparative efficacy remains unproven.

1. Executive synthesis

This run tested a proposed Cyclic Multi-Role Research Protocol (CMRP) by using the protocol on itself. The same GPT-5.6 Sol instance executed eleven frozen roles: planning, corpus archaeology, consolidated literature review, bleeding-edge contradiction search, source curation, evidence analysis, architecture reconciliation, experimental design, adversarial review, revision delta and final audit. The run demonstrates workflow feasibility and produced material self-corrections, but it does not prove comparative efficacy or epistemic independence.

The strongest conclusion is narrower than the starting hypothesis: same-model role cycling should be treated as structured research process control / test-time compute, not as a substitute for independent multi-agent or human review. Its plausible benefits are decomposition, provenance, explicit falsification and auditability. Its principal weakness is correlated error: the same model chooses queries, interprets evidence and critiques prior outputs.

2. Problem and objectives

Large tare.tools research sessions combine archaeology, web research, architecture, experiment design and adversarial review. A monolithic pass can hide assumptions, silently rewrite earlier conclusions and overcount a model's own review as independent evidence. True subagents are not available in this runtime, so the studied question is whether functional roles executed sequentially by one model can improve the process without misrepresenting the epistemology.

Objectives: measure what staged decomposition can legitimately provide; define explicit assurance ceilings; preserve role-level provenance and revision deltas; identify failure modes; and design a future benchmark against compute-matched and truly independent alternatives.

4. Internal corpus anchors

The method aligns with existing corpus principles: Research → Findings → ADR/SPEC is a separate promotion path; EvidenceFamily prevents nominal reviewer count from becoming false independence; evaluator metrology is preferable to trust by label; negative evidence must survive; and later passes should not silently erase earlier artifacts. The Research Frontier already distinguishes Pointer, Research Question, Hypothesis, Study and Finding, giving CMRP a natural input/output boundary.

5. Consolidated evidence

Self-Refine [R01] demonstrates that the same model can improve outputs through iterative feedback/refinement in several tasks, while Reflexion [R02] shows the value of explicit reflective artifacts when feedback is tied to task outcomes. SOP/role frameworks such as MetaGPT [R06] and Google's AI co-scientist [R09] provide precedent for functional decomposition and iterative scientific roles.

However, the evidence is task-dependent. The TACL critical survey [R03] finds little robust support for intrinsic prompted self-correction without reliable external feedback. Tyen et al. [R04] separate *finding* an error from correcting a known error; this is directly relevant to reviewer design. Yang et al. [R05] show that critique and confidence-preservation can trade off. The protocol therefore cannot assume that another self-critique pass monotonically improves correctness.

6. Bleeding edge and adversarial evidence

Controlled multi-agent debate work [R07] suggests intrinsic reasoning strength and diversity matter more than superficial debate settings; majority pressure can suppress correction. Okawa [R08] reports biased consensus and a protective role for heterogeneity in studied debate settings. Persona-assignment research [R13] further warns that personas can induce motivated reasoning. These results changed the proposed design: CMRP uses functional contracts and checklists, not role-play identities, and same-model roles remain one correlated epistemic family.

Critic/reviewer specialization work [R10–R12] also suggests that review quality should be treated as a measurable capability rather than inferred from general model strength or a prompt title.

7. The executed cyclic run

Run `CMRP-2026-08-11-001` materialized eleven role artifacts plus a hash manifest. The planner froze six hypotheses before external review. The corpus archaeologist established anti-drift boundaries. Two literature passes deliberately included contrary evidence. The evidence analyst rejected the claim that role count equals independence. The adversarial reviewer then produced nine material findings, forcing the revision delta to downgrade adoption, prohibit persona-as-diversity, add compute-matched baselines, protect prior correct claims from recency overwrite and declare a same-model audit assurance ceiling.

Because all interpretive passes used one model, the run is evidence of process execution, not evidence that the final scientific conclusions are independently correct.

8. ADOPT / ADAPT / RETIRE / OPEN

ADOPT for experiment: explicit role mission/forbidden moves; frozen intermediate artifacts; source identity pass; contrary-evidence search; adversarial pass; revision delta; final authority note.

ADAPT: “multi-agent research” → executor-neutral multi-role workflow; reviewer independence → four separate notions (functional separation, cognitive diversity, epistemic independence, authority independence); self-reflection → evidence-guided critique.

RETIRE: counting same-model roles as multiple EvidenceFamilies; persona theatrics as diversity; majority consensus as proof; same-model final audit as strict assurance; silent overwrite of earlier role outputs.

OPEN: fresh-context vs shared-context execution; optimal role/cycle depth; heterogeneous-review marginal value; query blinding; cross-disciplinary peer-review/team-cognition evidence.

9. Proposed protocol

A CMRP run begins with a ResearchPointer/question and freezes a planner artifact. It then executes independent-in-function passes for corpus archaeology, consolidated literature, bleeding-edge contradictions and source identity. Evidence analysis precedes architecture reconciliation. An experimental designer makes local claims falsifiable. An adversarial reviewer is required to search for reasons the synthesis is wrong. Revisions are recorded as deltas. The final audit declares its own independence class and assurance ceiling.

Stopping is not model satisfaction. A cycle ends when no new *material* evidence/contradiction appears, blocking checklist items are resolved or explicitly OPEN, a budget cap is hit, or independent escalation is required.

10. Falsifiable qualification program

Compare: A) single pass; B) compute-matched generic iterative refinement; C) same-model CMRP; D) isolated same-model multi-agent; E) heterogeneous multi-agent; F) heterogeneous + human/independent review. Use a stratified set of Research Frontier pointers and seed hidden contradictions, duplicate source manifestations, corrected/retracted sources, vendor overclaims, negative results, canonical-equivalent traps and false CURRENT statements.

Primary measures: supported-claim precision, contradiction recall, negative-evidence preservation, source-identity accuracy, architectural-duplication rate, false-correction rate, adversarial yield, residual unsupported-claim rate, cost-to-trust and calibration. Use paired repeated-measures and blinded final scoring.

12. Open Research Frontier

New pointers raised by this run: compute-matched qualification of CMRP, fresh-context vs shared-context role isolation, cross-disciplinary evidence from peer review/team cognition/Delphi/red-team methods, and functional-role evaluator qualification. These remain Research Frontier items rather than implementation tasks.

13. Limitations

This is an N=1 methodological demonstration. The same model designed the protocol, chose search queries, interpreted literature and performed the final screening audit. Web retrieval was not a registered systematic review. The evidence base is concentrated in LLM/NLP and agentic systems. No blinded human or heterogeneous-model evaluation was available. Accordingly, the run can justify experimental shadow use and a benchmark, not a claim of superior research quality.

14. Bibliography

  1. R01 — Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback. Madaan et al., 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651. Single-model feedback/refinement; positive evidence for iterative decomposition.
  2. R02 — Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning. Shinn et al., 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366. Verbal reflection grounded in task feedback and episodic memory.
  3. R03 — When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?. Kamoi et al., TACL 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/. Critical survey; intrinsic prompted self-correction is not robust in general and external feedback matters.
  4. R04 — LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location. Tyen et al., Findings ACL 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.826/. Separates mistake finding from correction.
  5. R05 — Confidence v.s. Critique. Yang et al., ACL 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.203/. Shows confidence/critique trade-offs during self-correction.
  6. R06 — MetaGPT. Hong et al., 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352. Role/SOP-based collaborative workflow precedent.
  7. R07 — Can LLM Agents Really Debate?. Wu et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784. Controlled debate: reasoning strength and diversity dominate; majority pressure can suppress correction.
  8. R08 — Emergence of Biased Consensus in Multi-Agent LLM Debates. Okawa, ICML 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02827. Interaction can amplify bias; heterogeneity suppresses biased consensus in studied conditions.
  9. R09 — AI co-scientist. Google Research, 2025. https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/. Industrial research-workflow precedent with specialized iterative roles; vendor evidence, not independent proof.
  10. R10 — CritiqueLLM. Ke et al., ACL 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.704/. Critique is a specialized capability that can be trained/evaluated.
  11. R11 — IF-CRITIC. Wen et al., ACL 2026. https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1147/. Fine-grained checklist-driven critic for instruction-following evaluation.
  12. R12 — OpenReviewer. Idahl & Ahmadi, NAACL 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-demo.44/. Specialized reviewer can be more critical than general-purpose LLMs; not a replacement for human review.
  13. R13 — Persona-Assigned Large Language Models Exhibit Human-Like Motivated Reasoning. Dash et al., 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20020. Persona assignment can induce motivated reasoning; warning against theatrical personas.
